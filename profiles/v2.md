# Hera Health Solutions — Capability Profile v2

*Sources: US patent (bioerodible drug delivery device), company technology page, company pipeline page. Corrected by hand from generated v1. Unverified items are flagged in Open Questions.*

## What they build

A drug delivery **platform**, not a drug. The core technology is a non-woven sheet of bioerodible polymer produced by **electrospinning** (solution concentration 1–100 mg/ml), blended with one or more active agents, then **rolled into multiple turns** along its length to form an implantable device. The rolled geometry is the mechanism for tuning release rate and degradation rate.

Key properties, in their own claims and copy:

- **Fully bioerodible** — the device degrades completely and requires **no surgical removal**, unlike incumbent non-degradable implants which must be excised once drug content is exhausted.
- **Constant-rate (zero-order) release** over **days, months, or years** — explicitly engineered against "fluctuating levels or uncontrollable **burst effects**."
- **Molecule-agnostic** — demonstrated with both **small molecules and large biologics** (preclinical).
- **Multi-API capable** — multiple active agents can be incorporated in a single device, enabling combination therapy.
- **Multiple form factors** — implants, **patches**, and other formats.
- Lead active agent named in patent claim 1: **etonogestrel** (the progestin used in Nexplanon) → long-acting contraception.
- Patent-stated application breadth: contraceptives, hormone therapies, chemotherapeutics, veterinary drugs.

**Business model: licensing.** They reformulate *existing, approved* molecules for pharmaceutical partners ("new ways for pharmaceutical companies to use proven and trusted molecules"), and solicit technology licensing.

## What they do NOT do

- **They do not discover drugs.** No novel molecules, no new APIs, no target identification, no hit-to-lead. They take proven, approved molecules and change how those molecules are delivered. This is the single most important boundary.
- **They do not run therapeutic-area science.** They are not a contraception company or an oncology company — those are applications of the platform, not their expertise.
- **They do not do non-degradable implants** — the whole premise is eliminating the removal procedure.
- **They do not do oral dosing.** Oral is the problem they exist to displace.
- **Not nanoparticle/liposomal delivery.** Their "nanotechnology" is nanofiber scaffolds — a different modality despite the shared prefix.
- **Not a device/hardware company** in the sense of pumps, catheters, or electronics.

## Fit criteria

*Framed in the problem language an agency would use — not in Hera's vocabulary.*

- The solicitation wants **sustained delivery of an existing approved drug** over weeks to years from a single administration.
- The stated problem is **adherence, compliance, or daily-dosing burden** — the clinical rationale for long-acting formats.
- The solicitation names a need for **controlled or zero-order release kinetics**, or explicitly cites **burst release** as a problem to solve.
- The solicitation wants a **long-acting injectable, depot, or implant** and is agnostic about the underlying technology. *(LAI is the agency's word for Hera's category — a competing incumbent format, but the same problem space.)*
- Removal, retrieval, or explantation of a device is framed as a burden or barrier.
- The solicitation seeks **reformulation, drug-device combination, or delivery platform** work rather than new chemistry.
- SBIR/STTR mechanisms (R43/R44, NSF SBIR), or platform/technology-development programs open to small businesses.

## Anti-fit signals

*Things that look relevant and are not.*

- **Novel drug discovery, target ID, or new API development** — the most common trap, because the disease area will match perfectly.
- **Clinical trial execution** of a therapeutic (they are a platform licensor, not a sponsor running trials).
- `implant` used in a **non-drug-delivery** sense: neural implants (BRAIN Initiative), cochlear, dental, orthopedic. Structurally identical word, unrelated technology.
- **Nanoparticle / liposome / LNP / microneedle** delivery — delivery, but not their delivery.
- **Oral, inhaled, or intranasal** reformulation.
- Solicitations wanting **therapeutic-area scientific expertise** (e.g. novel contraceptive mechanism) rather than delivery capability.
- Pure **materials science** with no drug-delivery endpoint.
- Broad `drug delivery` keyword matches that turn out to be substance-abuse or service-delivery grants.
